## Participation is a choice

You never need to date, create an app account, approach a stranger or discuss
your personal life for this course. Every exercise accepts a fictional case.
Every role-play has a solo written equivalent using the same criteria. Tell the
tutor which format you want; no explanation is required. Workshops are practice
and feedback opportunities, with no separate attendance mark.

## Respect and privacy

Ask before involving a classmate in a role-play, and accept a no immediately.
Do not treat the class as a pool of potential dates. Do not photograph, record
or share a classmate’s work without permission. Use invented names and details;
real dating profiles, private messages and screenshots do not belong in assessed
work. Discuss the choices in a case, not a classmate’s desirability.

Boyfriend, girlfriend and partner are equally welcome words here. Pink and blue
do not assign anyone a gender or a role. There is no requirement to be single,
seeking a relationship, or experienced in dating.

## Assessment and submission

The [three assessments](/assessments/) carry all course marks. The due dates are
shown on their pages; all deadlines are **5 pm Canberra time**. In this invented
course, submit a printed copy to the convenor or arrange an accessible digital
format in consultation. The public prototype has no login or upload service.

For this course’s fictional policy, request an extension from
[Mira Chen](/people/mira-chen/) before the deadline, suggesting a new date. If an
unexpected circumstance makes that impossible, speak to Mira at the next
consultation. Work up to seven calendar days late without an arranged extension
receives feedback with a cap of 50% of available marks. Beyond seven days, an
extension is needed for a mark. These are SLOP1276 design choices, not ANU policy.

Written feedback arrives within seven days for the first two assessments and
fourteen days for the final guide. To request a review, bring the marked work
and the criterion you want reconsidered to Mira within seven calendar days of
receiving the feedback. The review considers the same published criteria.

## Sources and AI assistance

Credit readings wherever they inform a decision. You may use AI to generate a
fictional case or propose alternative wording; add a short note naming the tool,
what it did, and what you changed. You remain responsible for the reasoning.
Never upload another person’s private messages or personal information to a tool.
Do not present generated conversations as real encounters or invent research
findings to justify advice.

## Access and support

Lecture notes and workshop tasks are available as text. You may answer in prose,
tables or a dialogue transcript. Ask Eli about a written workshop alternative,
and Mira about an assessment format or extension. See the
[teaching team](/people/) for fictional consultation times.

This course teaches everyday communication through cases. For real relationship
concerns, [love is respect](https://www.loveisrespect.org/) offers information
about boundaries and unhealthy behaviours. The Australian
[eSafety Commissioner](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/staying-safe/online-dating)
provides guidance about online dating and unwanted contact. These are external
resources, not SlopU services. You may stop an exercise or choose a different case
without sharing the reason.
