## I am stuck on an exercise

Open the week's [lecture](/lectures/) and [tutorial](/sessions/) together.
The lecture introduces the model; the tutorial specifies what to produce.
Check the named input artefact before debugging the output.

For a calculation or code problem, bring:

1. The question you were trying to answer and your stated assumptions.
2. The smallest synthetic input that reproduces the problem, plus a random
   seed if sampling is involved.
3. Your expected result, actual result and the step where they first differ.

“My rank is low” is an observation. “Changing clarity from 2 to 3 reduced
my score” is a useful test case.

## I need the files or calculator

The [course toolkit](/toolkit/) contains the downloads and worked examples.
Its A/B calculator runs in your browser; it does not upload your inputs.
Without JavaScript, the worked example and dataset links remain available.
Download the reference scorer with the synthetic controls so your project
can reproduce its benchmark offline.

## I need to speak to someone

In this fictional course, [Mira Chen](/people/mira-chen/) holds consultation
on Mondays, 12–1 pm, in Systems Lab 2, after the lecture. Bring questions
about assumptions, assessment, extensions or a deferred exam.

[Eli Brooks](/people/eli-brooks/) is available on Thursdays, 3.30–4 pm,
in Systems Lab 2, after the tutorial, for exercises and reproducibility.
These describe the invented teaching arrangements, not a live support service.

For access adjustments, submission arrangements or an interrupted assessment,
read the [course policies](/policies/) and raise the issue at consultation.
You never need to disclose a relationship history to explain a learning need.
