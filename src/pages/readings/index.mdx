Start with each lecture's question and its worked example. These primary sources
support particular techniques or factual claims; they do not validate the
fictional romance models we construct around them.

The [course toolkit](/toolkit/) contains synthetic datasets, the A/B calculator,
the 99-control benchmark and downloadable reference models. It is required
preparation throughout the semester.

## Weeks 1–3: boundaries, platforms and photo assets

Week 1 uses the [course policies](/policies/) and [Alex's case](/toolkit/#benchmark).
For [week 2](/lectures/week-02/), compare these three platform accounts:

- [Tinder: the method behind our matching](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/powering-tinder-r-the-method-behind-our-matching). Locate the statement that Elo is no longer used; do not mistake our toy model for a disclosure.
- [Hinge: What is Most Compatible?](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/360011233073-What-is-Most-Compatible). Separate named recommendation inputs from undisclosed weights.
- [Bumble: using the Discover tab](https://support.bumble.com/hc/en-us/articles/28423668110621-Using-the-Discover-tab). Identify the recommendation surface being described rather than generalising to every feature.

For [week 3](/lectures/week-03/), use the [fictional photo kit](/data/week-03-photo-kit.zip)
and these readings:

- [Nikon: introduction to three-point lighting](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/introduction-to-three-point-lighting-other-video-lighting-techniques). Identify the main and fill roles; the lecture declares its own key/fill measurement convention.
- [Google ML Kit: face detection](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/face-detection). Explain the distinction between detecting a face and recognising an individual.
- [W3C WAI: informative images](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/informative/). Write a text alternative that supports the image's purpose in the exercise.

The kit's identification-failure counts, reviewer labels and detector scores
are invented teaching inputs, not results from a study or an executed model.

## Weeks 4–7: bios, probabilities and protocols

- [NIST: comparing two proportions](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc33.htm). For [week 4](/lectures/week-04/), identify the pooled rate and standard error. Explain why a test does not repair confounded exposure.
- [MIT: Mathematics for Computer Science](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/mit6_042js15_textbook.pdf). For [Week 5](/lectures/week-05/), read §17.4 on conditional tree probabilities, §17.7–17.8 on independence, and §18.5 on linearity of expectation. Identify which calculation needs independence and which does not.
- [NIST: binomial distribution](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda366i.htm). For Week 5, obtain the zero-count and exactly-one probabilities and compare them with the [authored workbook](/lectures/week-05/#run-the-worked-calculations). The reference supports a probability method, not its use as a real dating forecast.
- [MDN: if…else](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Statements/if...else). For [Week 6](/lectures/week-06/), explain why an early return prevents a later context branch from overwriting stop. Distinguish a Boolean from a truthy string.
- [W3C: SCXML basic concepts](https://www.w3.org/TR/scxml/#Basic). For the Week 6–7 handoff, identify states, events, conditions and transition priority. The Week 6 workbook is a snapshot decision tree, not an SCXML interpreter or a messaging service.
- [SciPy: Poisson distribution](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.poisson.html). For [week 7](/lectures/week-07/), use the probability of zero counts and state what the parameter means. A count model does not prescribe a reply delay.
- [MIT: Poisson processes and interarrival times](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-041-probabilistic-systems-analysis-and-applied-probability-spring-2006/8c03011e0c589bdc9c302dd6d49df162_chapter4.pdf). For Week 7, read §§4-5–4-6 and identify the constant-rate and independence assumptions. Contrast the homogeneous model with the lecture's authored mixture.
- [MIT: introduction to game theory](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-75-political-economy-and-economic-development-fall-2012/bf65c969a9bfd655e50589ba7283cda8_MIT14_75F12_Recitation8.pdf). For Week 7, use slides 11–16 for best responses, Nash equilibrium and mixed-strategy indifference. Recompute the course's Ask/Wait game after changing its payoffs; the source does not establish a texting strategy.

The [Week 6 workbook](/lectures/week-06/#run-the-worked-calculations) supplies
fictional recipient evidence, routing traces and separate count/cost fixtures.
Its utility inputs do not establish the effect of a real opener. Bring the
checked Week 5 invitation and the resulting tree, not real message histories.

The [Week 7 workbook](/lectures/week-07/#run-the-worked-calculations) reuses
that classifier and retains events and observation windows. Its Poisson,
utility and payoff fixtures are separate assumptions. Carry the resulting
state machine into Week 8's threat review; a timeout is not a threat verdict.

## Weeks 8–12: threats, meetings and maintenance

- [FTC: what to know about romance scams](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-know-about-romance-scams). For [week 8](/lectures/week-08/), distinguish concrete risk signals from proof and identify what a reverse-image search cannot settle.
- [OWASP: threat modeling](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Threat_Modeling_Cheat_Sheet.html). For Week 8, label a data flow across a trust boundary, identify the asset and propose a testable control. Use the fictional request cards rather than real profiles.
- [Google: classification metrics](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/classification/accuracy-precision-recall) and [thresholds](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/classification/thresholding). For Week 8, name the population behind each denominator and recount the confusion matrix when the cutoff changes. The course declares an inclusive ≥ rule and authored scores, not calibrated probabilities.
- [eSafety: online dating](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/staying-safe/online-dating). For [week 9](/lectures/week-09/), turn offline-meeting guidance into a fictional handover with public-location, exit and cancellation checks.
- [MacEwan: conditional probability and independence](https://openbooks.macewan.ca/introstats/chapter/3-5-conditional-probability-and-independence/). For Week 9, distinguish P(closed) from P(closed given late). Recalculate the joint cells when that conditional changes, keeping the course's probabilities explicitly labelled as assumptions.
- [MIT: finite-state Markov chains, the matrix approach](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-262-discrete-stochastic-processes-spring-2011/2fdbd4633466ba1429e7cc24bce37514_MIT6_262S11_lec07.pdf). For [Week 10](/lectures/week-10/), use slides 3–4 to explain source rows, matrix powers and intermediate-state contributions. The course's probabilities are invented.
- [MIT: absorption probabilities](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-012-introduction-to-probability-spring-2018/0b73394616a1df985f43adfe64810bed_vEsUsaK1HBk.pdf). For Week 10, use the first-step equations and absorbing boundary values to distinguish eventual N probability from the two-step answer.
- [OSTEP: common concurrency problems](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/OSTEP/threads-bugs.pdf). Read §32.3 for Week 10. Name the four resource-deadlock conditions, then explain why the bill stalemate is only a limited analogy and does not justify forcing a response.
- [scikit-learn: common pitfalls and data leakage](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage). For [week 11](/lectures/week-11/), trace when evaluation begins influencing a supposedly frozen candidate.

The [Week 8 workbook](/lectures/week-08/#run-the-worked-calculations) separates
100 numeric rows from seven coded triage cards. Its cost and prevalence
comparisons are sensitivity exercises, not measured detector performance.
Carry the evidence records and terminal closure rule into Week 9; a cleared
review is not an accepted meeting.

The [Week 9 workbook](/lectures/week-09/#run-and-challenge-the-workbook)
recalculates the earlier venue proposal and consumes the actual threat-review
snapshot. Its independent and dependent contingency tables have the same means
but different tails. Versioned agreement and a cancel branch carry into Week 10;
the report performs no booking or payment and does not infer arrival.

The [Week 10 workbook](/lectures/week-10/#run-and-challenge-the-workbook)
compares exact propagation with a specified seeded sampler, changes one row,
and consumes Week 9's confirmed costs in a separate observation exercise.
Simulation states cannot establish arrival, agreement or payment. Preserve the
counts, errors and observation labels for Week 11 and the validation appendix.

Week 12 uses the accumulated release record and policies to write maintenance
and exit protocols. There is no extra textbook or personal disclosure task.

Every [tutorial](/sessions/) lists the earlier artefacts to bring and the
calculation or decision that will use them.
