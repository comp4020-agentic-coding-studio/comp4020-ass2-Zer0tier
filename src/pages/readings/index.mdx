Start with the relevant lecture page: it names the question you should bring
to the reading. These sources support the semester's models; none is a manual
for making another person like you.

The [course toolkit](/toolkit/) holds the synthetic datasets, A/B calculator,
99-profile benchmark and downloadable reference scorer used in tutorials.

## Weeks 1–3: what was actually measured?

- [Hinge: What is Most Compatible?](https://help.hinge.co/hc/en-us/articles/360011233073-What-is-Most-Compatible)
  Read alongside [week 1](/lectures/week-01/). Separate what the platform
  discloses from what you would have to infer. A missing equation is not
  permission to invent one.
- [Tinder: the method behind our matching](https://www.tinderpressroom.com/powering-tinder-r-the-method-behind-our-matching)
  Compare its account with the toy Elo model in week 1. Treat this as a
  platform's description, not an independent audit of its implementation.
- [NIST: comparing two proportions](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc33.htm)
  For [week 3](/lectures/week-03/), find the pooled proportion and standard
  error. Explain why changing an exposure count can change the conclusion
  without changing the observed percentage-point difference.

## Weeks 4–7: a model needs a boundary

- [scikit-learn: common pitfalls and data leakage](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/common_pitfalls.html#data-leakage)
  Read for [week 4](/lectures/week-04/), then revisit in
  [week 9](/lectures/week-09/). Trace the moment an evaluation result starts
  influencing a design decision. Renaming the data `test` does not undo it.
- [Gale and Shapley: College Admissions and the Stability of Marriage (PDF)](https://www.or.mist.i.u-tokyo.ac.jp/takeda/FreshmanCourse/GaleShapley.pdf)
  For [week 6](/lectures/week-06/), focus on the definition of stability and
  the proposal procedure. Hand-trace our four-by-four example before trying
  to prove anything about it. “Stable” has a precise meaning here.

## Weeks 8–12: simulation is not observation

- [NumPy: Generator.binomial](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/random/generated/numpy.random.Generator.binomial.html)
  Use with [week 8](/lectures/week-08/) and
  [week 10](/lectures/week-10/). Locate `n`, `p` and `size`; state which values
  you assumed rather than measured. The model can be implemented in another
  language or a spreadsheet.

The later weeks reuse these ideas to evaluate and release one coherent project.
There is no separate reading hurdle: preparation and the artefact to bring are
listed on each [tutorial page](/sessions/).
