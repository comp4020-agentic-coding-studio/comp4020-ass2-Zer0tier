## 90-minute tutorial

Recount the separate dashboard's **25% among all** versus **50% among resolved** records. Neither “nice time” nor a simulation draw supplies an agreement.
2. **25 minutes — evaluate frozen work.** Freeze exact text, ratings, evidence and versions. Run the primary score and conservative rank against all 99 controls; save that result before inspecting alternatives. The reference (4,4,3,4) scores **93.75, rank 2/100** because one control ties. 3. **20 minutes — change the conditions.** Double feasibility's weight for **all 100 entries**, dividing by 20. The library plan requires 100 minutes, and the unchanged bio contradicts the new window: case fidelity fails despite rank 2. Keep the primary result, common-weight sensitivity and candidate-only evidence change separate.
4. **25 minutes — release review and quiz.** Use fifteen minutes to review the release prepared before class: eight checking its labelled fictional page, exact frozen text, evaluation link, keyboard use, **1920×1080** and **390×844**, and resize; seven recording actual results, reproducing into a second new directory and comparing output bytes. ## Checkpoints and debrief

- **Observation:** before 1086 the Week 10 branch is pending; its latest completed record is agreed and the meeting ended. A withdrawal remains visible. A declined invitation terminates regardless of assumed utility.
- **Evidence:** each rating has a quote and reason. A quote-presence check cannot validate the rating; missing venue detail stays missing even when a notebook plan exists.
- **Evaluation:** report score, number strictly above, ties, rank and denominator. Do not round before ranking or compare new-weight candidates with old-weight controls. Empty resolved denominators are undefined, not zero.
- **Changed case:** controls contain no source bios, so do not invent re-ratings for their availability. A newly worded invitation needs a new version and review. An impressive rank cannot override a failed constraint.
- **Release:** the primary failed target stays visible; all sixteen inputs and output files have a retained checksum record. Browser evidence identifies what was actually inspected. A checksum establishes byte identity against that record, not authorship, accessibility or consent.
