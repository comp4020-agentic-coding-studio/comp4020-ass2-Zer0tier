## Before you arrive

Bring your conversation transcript and boundary statements. Identify the exact point where an invitation could fit, or explain why it would not.

Read [this week’s lecture](/lectures/week-06/) for the worked example.

Bring forward: [Boundary statements](/sessions/02-boundaries/), [Conversation transcript](/sessions/05-conversation/).

## In the room · 90 minutes

Draft one invitation in 20 minutes. Spend 30 minutes writing all three response branches. In 25 minutes, a peer or solo annotation checks for pressure, hidden assumptions and unclear details. Use 15 minutes for a final revision.

You can role-play with a willing classmate or write both sides of the fictional exchange on your own. Feedback concerns the choices on the page; nobody needs to describe their dating life.

## What leaves the room

**Invitation branches.** All three branches are complete; a refusal ends the invitation without bargaining.

After the teaching break, the yes branch becomes a first-date plan. There is no assessed work or required dating during the break.
