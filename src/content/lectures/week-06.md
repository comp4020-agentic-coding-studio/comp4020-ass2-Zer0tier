## The question

Could the other person tell what you are asking, and decline without managing your feelings?

## In this lecture

“We should hang out sometime” can mean several things. For this exercise, say what you mean: “I’ve enjoyed talking. Would you like to go on a coffee date with me on Thursday afternoon?” Specificity gives the other person an actual choice.

An invitation has three useful pieces: an activity, a time or a request to find one, and language that makes the interest clear. It is not an invoice for the conversation you just had. A friendly exchange creates no obligation to accept.

Write three branches. If Sam says yes, Alex checks practical details. If Sam says no, Alex accepts it: “Thanks for letting me know. See you at the next game.” If Sam says they are unsure, Alex leaves the next step with Sam. Repeated invitations do not turn uncertainty into agreement.

Use the boundary statements from week 2 to check your wording. Then use the week 5 transcript to decide whether there is an appropriate moment to ask. Sometimes the best revision is to finish the conversation without an invitation.

## Try it before the workshop

Bring your conversation transcript and boundary statements. Identify the exact point where an invitation could fit, or explain why it would not.

Bring your draft to [week 6’s workshop](/sessions/06-invitations/). After the teaching break, the yes branch becomes a first-date plan. There is no assessed work or required dating during the break.
