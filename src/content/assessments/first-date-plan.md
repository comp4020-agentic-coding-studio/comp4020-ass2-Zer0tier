## The brief

Alex and Sam have enjoyed talking at a board-game afternoon. Alex would like a date. Sam uses step-free venues, prefers quiet spaces and has a limited budget. Alex has an evening shift. Write the invitation, every answer branch, and the plan if both choose to meet.

Build on your intentions map. If you used another fictional character, carry their constraints into this brief and state what changed.

## What you submit

One PDF of 800–1,000 words. Include a short introduction, an eight-turn conversation excerpt, one invitation, separate responses to yes/no/unsure, and a first-date plan. The plan must cover agreement, access, cost, travel, duration and what happens if the venue is unavailable.

Add a 150-word rationale linking two decisions to your earlier intentions-map feedback. Refer to [eSafety’s online dating guidance](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/staying-safe/online-dating) when discussing first-meeting precautions; explain its application rather than pasting a checklist.

## Preparation and feedback

Use your [introduction card](/sessions/04-introductions/), [conversation transcript](/sessions/05-conversation/), [invitation branches](/sessions/06-invitations/) and [week 7 plan](/sessions/07-first-date/). Feedback is returned within seven days, ready to use in your final guide.

## Submission

Hand a printed copy to the convenor by 5 pm Canberra time on the due date, or arrange an accessible digital format in consultation. This is the invented course’s submission method; the prototype has no upload service. [Course policies](/policies/) apply.

Everything can be written alone using the fictional case. You do not need to ask anyone out, create an app profile or go on a date. Marks reward the decisions and their explanation.
