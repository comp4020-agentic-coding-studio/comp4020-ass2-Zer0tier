## The brief

Jo and Riley meet through friends, live an hour apart and enjoy one date. Jo wants regular weekend time; Riley is moving away at the end of semester. Build a guide that helps them decide what to do next. Then change one fact: Riley is staying. Explain what changes in the guide and what does not.

Your guide must cover the route from intentions to meeting, conversation, invitation, a first date, uncertainty, compatibility and a relationship conversation. It can end with becoming partners, continuing to learn about each other, or choosing to stop.

## What you submit

One PDF of 1,600–2,000 words with a contents list and clearly named sections. Reuse revised earlier work where it fits. Include a connected main path, a stopping branch after a refusal, a six-turn disagreement dialogue, and two possible endings to the relationship conversation.

Finish with a 250-word change note: identify one revision from each earlier assessment’s feedback, then explain the changed Jo and Riley case. Cite outside readings at the point where you use them. No real messages, identifying details or proof of dating belong in the guide.

## What makes the guide hold together

The intention at the start should still matter to the final choice. The first-date plan must respect the boundaries you stated. A refusal cannot lead to advice to keep asking. A polished set of unrelated worksheets will not meet the coherence criterion.

The [week 8 decision tree](/sessions/08-uncertainty/), [week 9 questions](/sessions/09-compatibility/), [week 10 dialogue](/sessions/10-disagreement/) and [week 11 conversation](/sessions/11-define-it/) supply the later branches. [Week 12](/sessions/12-field-guide/) is a full draft review.

## Submission and feedback

Hand the printed guide to the convenor by 5 pm Canberra time on the due date, or arrange an accessible digital format. This prototype has no submission service. Written feedback is returned within fourteen days. [Course policies](/policies/) cover extensions and review.

No marks depend on finding a boyfriend, girlfriend or partner. A carefully justified decision to remain single can meet every criterion.
