## Exercise · compute means and exceedance

**7 minutes.** Check probability mass sums to one.

Multiply each complete cost and duration by its probability, then add.

Which rows exceed **110 minutes**?

Which exceed Alex's **120-minute window** or **$20 budget**?

---

## Answer · the original numerical contract

Expected cost: **$14.60**. Expected duration: **96 minutes**.

Only late-and-closed exceeds 110 minutes: **2.5%**.

No supplied row exceeds 120 minutes or $20. Equality passes.

The four-state table is an exact model calculation, not an estimate from actual journeys.

---

## Additive means use the marginals

K = **14 + 6I(C)**. T = **90 + 20I(L) + 10I(C)**.

E(K) = **14 + 6P(C) = 14.60**.

E(T) = **90 + 20P(L) + 10P(C) = 96**.

I is a 0/1 event indicator. These expectation equations do not require independence between L and C.

## A mean does not specify a tail

Expected 96 minutes is not a promise that every outcome finishes in 96.

It is also not a 96% probability.

Here **T > 110** occurs only when **L and C** both occur.

That event depends on their joint distribution, which the two marginals do not uniquely determine.

## Exercise · test the exact boundary

**4 minutes.** Under independence, compare strict time targets **109, 110 and 120** minutes.

Add the probabilities of the rows exceeding each target.

What does an erroneous ≥ comparison report at 110?

State why a $20 outcome passes the budget check.

---

## Answer · equality changes the event

Strict tails: **25% at 109**, **2.5% at 110**, **0% at 120**.

At 110, ≥ wrongly includes the late/open row and reports **25%**.

Cost exactly $20 is permitted; cost > $20 is not.

The reference function combines time or budget exceedance. Here no row exceeds the budget.

## Replace independence with conditionals

Assume **P(C | L) = 0.4**, **P(C | not L) = 0**.

Keep **P(L) = 0.25**.

P(C) = **0.25 × 0.4 + 0.75 × 0 = 0.10**.

[MacEwan: conditional probability and independence](https://openbooks.macewan.ca/introstats/chapter/3-5-conditional-probability-and-independence/)

## Let the unknown joint probability be q

q = **P(L and C)**, with marginals 0.25 and 0.10.

On/open: **0.65 + q**. On/closed: **0.10 − q**.

Late/open: **0.25 − q**. Late/closed: **q**.

Every cell must be non-negative. Hence **0 ≤ q ≤ 0.10**.

---

## Exercise · reach both endpoints

**5 minutes.** Find P(C | L) and P(C | not L) when q is **0** and **0.10**.

Check both marginals remain unchanged.

Where is independence in this interval?

What range of >110-minute tails can these marginals support?

---

## Answer · marginals leave a range

Conditionals: **q/0.25** and **(0.10 − q)/0.75**.

At q = 0: **0 and 2/15**. At q = 0.10: **0.4 and 0**.

Independence: q = **0.025**, both conditionals **0.10**.

The tail can range from **0% to 10%** within this binary consequence model.
