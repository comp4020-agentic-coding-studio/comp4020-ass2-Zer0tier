## Conditional is doing work here

**P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B | A)**

Here: **0.30 × 0.20 = 0.06**.

The marginal P(B) is **13/100**, not 6/30. Multiplying the marginals gives 0.039 and the wrong joint probability.

[MIT: conditional product rule, §17.4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/mit6_042js15_textbook.pdf)

---

## Answer · the arrow is not reversible

P(B) = **13/100**. P(B | A) = **6/30 = 20%**.

P(A | B) = **6/13 ≈ 46.15%**.

P(A or B) = **37/100**. Count the six mutual pairs once.

The events are dependent: 0.20 ≠ 0.13. With zero A selections, P(B | A) is undefined; the joint count is zero.

---

## Twenty opportunities: what is X?

Let **Iᵢ = 1** for a mutual match on opportunity i, otherwise 0.

**X = I₁ + … + I₂₀** is a count, not a probability.

The repeated model generates new pair opportunities. It does not draw the table's rows without replacement.

---

## Expectation adds, even with dependence

**E[X] = Σ P(Mᵢ)**

Twenty marginal probabilities of 0.06 give **E[X] = 1.2**.

An expectation can be fractional. The realised count cannot.

Linearity needs no independence between opportunities. The individual probabilities still need justification.

[MIT: linearity of expectation, §18.5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-042j-mathematics-for-computer-science-spring-2015/mit6_042js15_textbook.pdf)

---

## At least one: use the complement

With **independent** match indicators and the **same p = 0.06**:

P(X = 0) = **0.94²⁰ ≈ 0.2901**.

P(X ≥ 1) = **1 − 0.94²⁰ ≈ 70.99%**.

This model leaves about a 29% chance of zero. It does not book 1.2 meetings.

## Answer · same mean, different chance

Zero opportunities: expected **0**, chance of one **0**.

Twenty opportunities at p = 0: **0 and 0**; at p = 1: **20 and 1**.

Shared draw: X = 20 with probability 0.06, otherwise X = 0.

**E[X] = 1.2**, but **P(X ≥ 1) = 6%**. Dependence changes the distribution.

## Unequal probabilities need their own product

For independent events: **P(any) = 1 − ∏(1 − pᵢ)**.

Ten at 0.02 and ten at 0.10 give **E[X] = 1.2** and **P(any) ≈ 71.51%**.

Replacing every pᵢ with the mean 0.06 gives 70.99% instead.

Equal means do not imply equal distributions.

## Two different independence questions

**Within a pair:** is P(B | A) equal to P(B)? The table says no.

**Across opportunities:** are M₁, …, M₂₀ mutually independent? The repeated toy model assumes yes.

You may use conditional selection within each pair and independent match indicators across pairs. State both choices.
