## Count a declared event

The toy arrival model counts **inbound messages**, including declines.

Send, delivery and read receipts are not inbound-message arrivals.

Assume a homogeneous Poisson process with **λ = 0.4 events/hour**.

The state traces are separate fixtures; six authored examples cannot validate this rate.

---

## Rate times duration gives a mean count

μ = λt = **0.4 × 2 = 0.8 events**.

P(N = k) = exp(−μ) × μᵏ / k!

P(N = 0) = **exp(−0.8) ≈ 0.449329**.

[SciPy: Poisson count distribution](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.poisson.html)

## A count is not a reply deadline

Expected count **0.8** does not mean **80% chance** of at least one arrival.

That probability is **1 − exp(−0.8) ≈ 55.07%**.

A count may exceed one. A probability cannot.

Neither number identifies when someone should reply or when another message is appropriate.

## Answer · keep the event definitions

Zero: **0.449329**. At least one: **0.550671**.

Exactly one: **0.8 × exp(−0.8) ≈ 0.359463**.

At rates 0.2 and 0.8, zero-count probabilities are **0.670320** and **0.201897**.

At zero rate or duration, zero arrivals has probability 1. Negative or non-finite inputs are invalid.

## The process assumptions do work

Constant rate; independent counts in disjoint intervals.

Work, sleep, notifications and clustered turns are omitted structure.

After zero in the first two hours, the next two still have zero probability exp(−0.8) **under this process**.

## One decision maker, three options

Clarify: **7q − 5**. Wait: **2q − 1**. Stop: **0**.

q is an assumed probability that clarification would be welcome.

Costs and coefficients are arbitrary utility units.

This is a decision against uncertainty; the other person is not yet a strategic player in this model.

---

## Positive utility is not enough

Clarify beats stop when **q > 5/7**.

Clarify beats wait when **5q > 4**, or **q > 0.8**.

It must beat both.

At q = 0.75, clarify scores 0.25 but wait scores 0.50. A positive score can still lose.

## Declare the double-text penalty

Let d ≥ 0 be an **assumed extra cost**.

U(C) = 7q − 5 − d.

Clarify beats wait only if **q > 0.8 + d/5**.

At d = 1, strict preference is impossible. This is sensitivity to a parameter, not a measured social law.

## Find both players' best responses

Fix B = Ask: A receives **1 for Ask, 0 for Wait**.

Fix B = Wait: A receives **2 for Ask, 0 for Wait**.

Ask is better against either opposing action: **strict dominance**.

Use B's second coordinate to repeat the check. B also strictly prefers Ask against either action.

---

## Equilibrium is a joint check

A Nash equilibrium puts **both players on best responses**.

Neither improves by changing alone while the other choice stays fixed.

Here the unique equilibrium is **(Ask, Ask)**, because Ask strictly dominates for both.

[MIT: best responses and Nash equilibrium](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-75-political-economy-and-economic-development-fall-2012/bf65c969a9bfd655e50589ba7283cda8_MIT14_75F12_Recitation8.pdf)

## Exercise · repair the dominance claim

Does Ask still strictly dominate Wait?

---

## Answer · two pure equilibria remain

Against Ask, choose **Wait: 0 beats −1**.

Against Wait, choose **Ask: 2 beats 0**.

The equilibria are **(Ask, Wait)** and **(Wait, Ask)**.

At Ask/Ask, either player can improve by waiting. At Wait/Wait, either can improve by asking. Neither action strictly dominates.

---

## Mix only after calculating indifference

Let r be the other player's Ask probability in the changed game.

Expected Ask payoff: **−r + 2(1 − r) = 2 − 3r**.

Wait payoff: **0**. Equalise them: **r = 2/3**.

By symmetry, both choosing Ask with probability 2/3 form a mixed equilibrium. Both actions then yield zero expected utility.

---

## A game result is not a texting script

r is a strategy probability; q is the separate welcome assumption; λ is an arrival rate.

The mixed calculation does not remove the two pure equilibria.

Payoffs are invented. Equilibrium does not establish consent or predict behaviour.

Do not turn 2/3 into a rule for randomising real messages.

## Answer · one ends a window, one closes contact

Timeout trace: **replied / no-reply**, with late-content review.

Refusal trace: **closed / reply**, with closure reason decline.

The decline itself counts as an inbound reply before the deadline. Later events leave closed unchanged.

A local close before the deadline without a reply gives **ended**, not a completed no-reply window.

## Duplicate records need an identity rule

Same event ID, type and time → **no-op**.

Same ID with different content → **conflict for review**.

A new second-send ID is rejected. Old timestamps on new events are rejected too.

This works only with retained state and IDs. It is not an exactly-once delivery guarantee.

---

## Check expiry before classifying the reply

If an open observation reaches **time ≥ deadline**, freeze no-reply.

Then process the supplied event.

A late reply is late even when no explicit timeout event came first.

Nothing wakes up automatically: replay progresses only when the caller supplies an event.
