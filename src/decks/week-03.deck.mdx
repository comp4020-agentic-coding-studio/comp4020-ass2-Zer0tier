## Key and fill have different jobs

<PhotoFigure kind="lighting" />

[Nikon's lighting introduction](https://www.nikonusa.com/learn-and-explore/c/tips-and-techniques/introduction-to-three-point-lighting-other-video-lighting-techniques) describes a main light and a fill that reduces shadows.

---

## Declare what your ratio measures

For this exercise: **key-to-fill = key reading / fill reading**.

Use the same unit and measurement setup for both readings.

**200 / 100 = 2:1**.

This is our source-reading convention. It is not a measured bright-side/shadow-side ratio or a match predictor.

## Change the fill · 6 min

Keep the key reading at **200**. Change fill from **100 to 50**.

Calculate the new ratio. Then double both original readings.

What does each change preserve?

Finally, try fill = 0. Decide what the calculation should report.

---

## Lighting answers

Halve fill: **200 / 50 = 4:1**.

Double both: **400 / 200 = 2:1**; the ratio is unchanged.

Fill = 0: the finite ratio is **undefined**. Flag it explicitly.

A ratio alone cannot reconstruct the image's exposure, colour or background.

## Design one controlled comparison · 5 min

Hypothesis: changing fill changes visible shadow detail in a chosen setup.

Specify what stays fixed: subject, pose, framing, background and camera settings.

Change one light input; define how shadow detail will be judged.

A proposed procedure using drawings is sufficient. This is not a dating experiment.

---

## A useful capture record

Save the setup ID, source file, camera settings if applicable, key/fill readings and units.

Record the changed input and the annotation rule before comparison.

Our supplied readings support arithmetic only; they contain no measured shadow-detail outcomes.

Do not announce that the hypothesis passed without observations.

## A crop is a transformation

<PhotoFigure kind="crop-a" />

Record **x, y, width and height** in the original coordinate system.

---

## Count the retained area

A's source is **600 × 400** coordinate units.

Its crop is **300 × 400**, starting at **(150, 0)**.

Retained fraction = **120,000 / 240,000 = 50%**.

Half the area remains. That is not a 50% information score; inspect what the crop preserves.

---

## Inspect the rectangle · 8 min

Use the A comparison: does the Alex label remain? Do the game tiles remain?

Check **x + width ≤ source width** and **y + height ≤ source height**.

Now try **x = 450, width = 300** on the same source.

Decide whether this proposed crop is valid before discussing its style.

---

## Crop debrief

Original crop: **150 + 300 = 450 ≤ 600**; height ends at 400.

The supplied label and game tiles stay visible.

Changed crop: **450 + 300 = 750 > 600**. Reject the rectangle.

Area arithmetic checks geometry. The image and annotation check meaning.

## Reproduce the rates · 6 min

Open `identification-trials.csv` in the [photo kit](/data/week-03-photo-kit.zip).

For each category, count reviewer 1 failures and divide by that category's trial count.

Check unique trial IDs and labels in **{0, 1}**.

State the event and denominator beside every percentage.

---

## Two datasets, two denominators

**Manifest:** one ambiguous crop among **three assets** → **1/3**.

**Trial file:** six identification failures among **ten group trials** → **6/10**.

The assets and trials are separate fixtures. Do not join them as if they describe the same images.

Crop ambiguity and identification failure are different labels.

---

## A pooled number needs a frame

Reviewer 1 marks **6 + 2 + 3 = 11** failures across **30** trials.

Pooled failure fraction = **11/30 ≈ 36.7%**.

The three groups contribute equally here. With unequal trial counts, use total failures / total trials.

The result describes this constructed fixture, not photographs in general.

---

## Same totals, different labels

<PhotoFigure kind="agreement" />

Each reviewer reports **six failures**. Are their annotations identical?

---

## Compare the reviewers · 4 min

Find every disagreement among G01–G10.

Compute **agreements / trials**.

Write one clarification that could help reviewers apply the subject-identification rule consistently.

If they agree, have they necessarily identified the subject correctly?

---

## Agreement debrief

Disagreements: **G06 and G07**.

Agreement = **8 / 10 = 80%**.

Both reviewers have six failures, but on different trials.

Agreement measures consistency between these labels, not correctness. A supported reference annotation is a separate requirement.

---

## One label can move a small rate

In a separate sensitivity copy, change reviewer 1's **G06 from failure to pass**.

Group failures fall from **6/10 to 5/10**: **60% → 50%**.

Keep the baseline and record the reason for the revision.

Invented trials cannot establish a universal ranking of photo categories.

---

## Build a manifest, not a beauty score

**Origin:** asset ID, source and exercise permission status.

**Transformation:** original size, crop rectangle and revision.

**Meaning:** intended subject, supporting cue, ambiguity and alt text.

**Review:** decision, reasons and the exact file selected.

---

## Alt text carries the relevant information

[W3C's informative-image guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/images/informative/) asks for a text alternative that conveys the image's purpose and content.

Here, the subject label and visible crop matter to the exercise.

“Photo” loses that information. “Attractive and trustworthy” invents an assessment.

Describe what the reader needs for this task.

---

## Alt-text worked answers

**A:** “Teaching illustration of fictional Alex, labelled on a blue top, behind a table with three game tiles.”

---

## Apply the acceptance checks

Require a recorded source, exercise permission, supported intended subject and valid crop.

Keep crop ambiguity explicit and supply relevant alt text.

Block missing required information before selecting an asset.

A high detector score cannot compensate for a failed permission or identity check.

---

## Implement the audit · 8 min

Validate the three records, then return **pass/block plus reasons** for each.

Test a blank source and an out-of-bounds crop. Neither should pass.

Retain blocked records. Select from the eligible set, then freeze the file and metadata.

The downloadable workbook supplies assertions after your own attempt.

---

## Selection: worked answer

**A passes:** supplied identity, permission record, valid crop and useful description.

**B blocked:** unresolved subject and ambiguous crop.

**C blocked:** unknown exercise permission and obscured subject.

One of three crops is ambiguous, yet only one of three assets passes. Those fractions answer different questions.
