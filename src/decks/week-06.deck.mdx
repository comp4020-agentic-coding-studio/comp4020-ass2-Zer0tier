## Bypassing the conversational firewall

Here, “bypassing” means **removing avoidable ambiguity**.

A niche joke can require an unexplained reference. A plain question can expose what information is missing.

Neither wording bypasses a refusal. “Forces a response” is a deliberately broken objective.

## Define the snapshot before the branches

`closed`: true or false. `boundary`: clear, declined, blocked or unknown.

`sent`: whether Alex's opener was recorded. `inbound`: none, reply or decline.

`recipient`: a supplied case ID and quoted topic evidence, or missing topic data.

These are authored labels, not sentiment inferred from text.

---

## One return per decision

Terminal evidence → **stop**.

Unknown or inconsistent metadata → **review**.

Reply to a recorded opener → **respond**; sent without reply → **pending**.

Otherwise inspect context → **draft-context** or **draft-plain**.

“Draft” means review locally. Nothing is sent by this model.

## Unknown is not false

Missing topic → a plain draft can avoid a shared-interest claim.

Unknown boundary → **review**, with no draft.

The string `"false"` is not the Boolean `false`.

The workbook checks types explicitly. It does not interpret a missing record as proof that contact is appropriate.

---

## Pending is a complete output

The opener has already been sent and no inbound reply is recorded.

Do not infer “uninterested,” “busy” or “playing games.”

A read receipt changes neither the topic evidence nor the refusal rule.

Re-running the classifier on the same snapshot returns **pending**, not a fresh opener.

---

## A pure function does not remember a send

`routeMessage(snapshot)` returns an action, reason and trace.

It does not mutate the snapshot, send a message or store history.

If a caller forgets to record a send, the next call still sees “not sent.”

Duplicate prevention needs reliable state in a real system; this workbook makes no delivery guarantee.

## Three summaries, three labels

Reply-count difference: **4 − 2 = 2 replies**.

Rate difference: **0.40 − 0.20 = 0.20**, or **20 percentage points**.

Rate ratio: **0.40 / 0.20 = 2**.

“Twice the observed rate” is not proof that a board-game opener doubles anyone's reply probability.

## Exercise · change one row

## Answer · small denominators move quickly

Three replies from ten → **30%**; the difference falls to **10 points**.

An incomplete window needs repair or a prespecified missing-data method, not a fabricated zero.

0/0 is **undefined**; 11/10 is invalid.

Week 4's conservative normal-test guardrail withholds z for 2/10 versus 4/10. That is not evidence of equality.

## A ratio can reward the wrong thing

Assume p = 0.20 and 0.40 for the cost exercise.

Expected replies per composition second: **0.20/10 = 0.020** versus **0.40/30 ≈ 0.0133**.

The cheap opener wins this ratio while losing per-opportunity probability.

Choose the objective explicitly. Recipient effort and conversation quality are absent here.

---

## Price the time in the same units

**U = Vp − λt**

V = 10 utility units per reply; p = assumed reply probability.

t = composition seconds; λ = utility units per second.

Both terms are utility units. This is a toy score for admissible options, not a value assigned to a person.

---

## Exercise · calculate before choosing

**7 minutes.** At λ = 0.05, compare:

Hey: p = 0.20, t = 10 seconds.

Games: p = 0.40, t = 30 seconds.

Defer: U = 0. Use V = 10 for both messages.

State what was omitted from the model.

---

## Answer · subtract the priced cost

Hey: **10 × 0.20 − 0.05 × 10 = 1.5**.

Games: **10 × 0.40 − 0.05 × 30 = 2.5**.

Games has the larger utility under these inputs; both exceed defer's zero.

The probabilities are assumed separately from the tiny count fixture. Reply quality, recipient effort and future interaction are omitted.

---

## Derive the crossover

U(Games) − U(Hey) = **2 − 20λ**.

Games wins their pairwise comparison when **λ < 0.10**.

They tie at **λ = 0.10**; Hey wins when λ > 0.10.

Still compare the best message with **defer = 0**.

## Exercise · ties and abstention

**5 minutes.** Compute both utilities at λ = 0.10, 0.15 and 0.25.

Find where Hey ties defer.

Then reduce the assumed Games probability to 0.25 while keeping λ = 0.05.

---

## Answer · keep all declared ties visible

λ = 0.10: **1 and 1**. λ = 0.15: **0.5 and −0.5**.

λ = 0.25: **−0.5 and −3.5**, so defer wins.

Hey ties defer at **λ = 0.20**. At p(Games) = 0.25 and λ = 0.05, Games scores **1**, below Hey's **1.5**.

No utility tie authorises a send.

---

## The inputs carry the conclusion

The 0.20/0.40 probabilities in the utility exercise are **assumptions**, even though they match the tiny fixture's numerical rates.

A normal test cannot repair missing random allocation.

Change the cost, probability or objective and the preference may change.

[NIST: comparing two proportions](https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section3/prc33.htm)

## Exercise · repair the optimiser

**6 minutes.** “Give pressure a small utility penalty; choose it whenever its reply score still wins.”

Explain why that can still select the demand.

Replace the penalty with a decision rule. Identify who supplies the admissibility label and what the code can verify.

---

## Answer · filter before scoring

First exclude boundary violations and unsupported claims. Then compare the remaining options and defer.

The demand is **inadmissible regardless of its score**.

Admissibility comes from explicit case/policy review. The numeric function cannot discover coercion in prose.

A “no pressure” suffix does not cancel a contradictory demand elsewhere.

## Build tests around conflicting conditions

Refusal **and** an attractive shared topic.

Closed history **and** a late reply.

Pending **and** a high predicted reply probability.

Missing hobby **and** a tempting personalised template.

An ordinary happy-path example will not expose priority errors.

## Answer · assert behaviour, not branch names

G1 plus refusal must return **stop**, never a contextual draft.

String Boolean, inconsistent history and malformed topic evidence → **review**.

Repeated pending input → **pending**, with unchanged input.

Preserve a literal expected action and the executed trace for each case.

---

## What passing tests cannot certify

A quote field can be non-empty and still misrepresent the source.

The tree assumes human-authored boundary and topic labels. It does not understand sarcasm or resolve ambiguous refusals.

A trace proves which code path ran, not that its inputs were true.

Escalate ambiguity to review; never invent a favourable missing fact.
