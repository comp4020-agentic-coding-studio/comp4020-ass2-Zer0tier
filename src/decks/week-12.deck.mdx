## A coded response needs an actor and target

An acceptance names the exact proposal and base version.

Duplicate delivery of the **same event** cannot add a vote. A new ID from the same actor cannot supply another person's response.

The last adopted terms remain separate from pending terms.

The code records authored acknowledgements; it proves no person's identity.

## The stale-copy problem

<MaintenanceFigure kind="stale" />

---

## Exercise · two copies started at v1

**5 minutes.** One change is adopted and creates v2. Another local copy submits a proposal based on v1.

Overwrite v2, silently rebase to v2, or reject the stale proposal?

What evidence would the first two choices invent or discard?

---

## Answer · review the actual current terms

Reject the stale write. Read v2 and its changes before making a separately reviewed proposal.

[RFC 9110: If-Match](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#name-if-match) supplies the software precondition analogy.

Our reducer is local. A distributed implementation needs an atomic check and update at its authority.

Ending never depends on a current base version.

## A deadline supplies no answer

## Exercise · what is terminal?

**5 minutes.** Compare three branches:

No final acceptance by tick 10.

B explicitly rejects the changed routine at tick 3.

B ends the agreement at tick 3.

Name the status of the proposal and the whole agreement.

---

## Answer · preserve the distinction

No response: proposal **expired**, still review.

Explicit no to the change: proposal **rejected**, still review. Do not retry its terms under a new ID.

End: whole scoped agreement **ended**.

Recorded old terms are not automatically enforced. Expiry is not an invitation to keep asking.

## Adopting and ending use different rules

<MaintenanceFigure kind="terminal" />

## Exercise · end during review

**5 minutes.** Only A has accepted a pending change. B ends the agreement.

Now deliver a late acceptance, a clock tick and an old-version restoration attempt.

Compare the **complete snapshot** after each input, not just the phase label.

---

## Answer · the terminal guard wins

The pending proposal becomes **cancelled-by-ending**. The agreement records **endedBy: B**.

Every later input leaves the complete ended record unchanged.

No second vote, up-to-date screen or settled practical task is required to end.

A new identifier cannot be used to bypass this boundary.

---

## Make the convention testable

Reject unknown actors, stale versions, conflicting IDs and backwards chronology.

Accept exact duplicate delivery once. Use **[1,10)** for this proposal window.

In a disposable copy, remove the ended guard or include tick 10. Predict the failing literal answer, run, restore and rerun.

A classroom ledger is not tamper-proof storage.

## The “comfortable rut” scenario

One fictional participant explicitly says: **“I want more choice than the same activity each time.”**

The maintenance objective is to make that change discussable.

Latency, facial expression or an emoji count does not supply the statement.

More monitoring is not automatically better maintenance.

---

## Exercise · measure what is needed

**5 minutes.** Compare collecting daily location, read-receipt and mood records with an optional check-in about activity choices.

What is the stated need? Which information serves it?

How can the participant decline the discussion or end the arrangement?

---

## Answer · a bounded change request

Ask about the activity choice directly, if the discussion is welcome.

Name scope, affected participants, proposed terms and the option to pass.

Surveillance cannot supply agreement.

“Would you like to discuss changing our usual activity? Fine to leave it for now or say no.” is an authored draft, not a sent message.

## Deprecate without a persuasion loop

## Exercise · the game is still borrowed

**5 minutes.** An agreement ends while a borrowed game and an unresolved expense remain.

Someone proposes keeping the relationship active until both are settled.

Repair the model and write one bounded practical task record.

---

## Answer · two independent statuses

**Agreement: ended.** Practical task: unresolved.

Record the item, owner, task and a separately acceptable method. Do not invent delivery or payment.

An unresolved task cannot require an in-person meeting or buy renewed agreement.

No protocol guarantees zero emotional pain.

Is its deletion task complete? What control do you have over another person's copy?

## Answer · record the remaining scope

Both own copies belong in the plan. The other person's copy stays **outside control**.

At day 30, two release dependencies require review rather than automatic deletion.

[W3C data minimization](https://www.w3.org/TR/privacy-principles/#data-minimization) supports limiting unnecessary data.

The 7/14/30-day periods are invented. The planner deletes no listed file and sends no request.

## Exercise · two misleading repairs

**5 minutes.** Repair A recalculates expected hashes from the faulty files.

Repair B copies only index.html back, leaving obsolete.txt.

Explain why both miss the stated restoration contract.

What changes if the rollback archive itself no longer matches the retained manifest?

---

## Answer · retain the expected answer

A changes the baseline to fit the fault. B leaves a stale extra output.

Restore the complete declared snapshot. Reject a changed archive.

[Git revert](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-revert) records a reversal as a new commit; this workbook uses local file snapshots.

Matching self-generated hashes cannot prove provenance if both files and hashes were replaced.

## Acceptance and quality remain separate

Acceptance: the fictional page opens, matches the evaluated text and reproduces its published result.

Quality: the explanation assesses evidence, scope, sensitivity and failed assumptions.

The final's rubric grades engineering and judgment.

It does not grade romantic outcomes or achieved benchmark rank.
