# Algorithmic Romance

The syllabus drop.

SLOP1276 · Week 01 · Alex's story

Fictional Alex's first bio says “Seeking synergistic engagement.” It could advertise a printer. A Friday invitation needs checking too.

**Investigate:** Which promises do the supplied facts support?

## By the end of today

Identify supplied **inputs and constraints**.

Separate an **observation** from an inference about hidden state.

We will practise all three with one fictional person: Alex.

---

## The opening provocation

**Claim to test:** “Love is an inefficient market problem solvable through data structures.”

A system can store availability and reject plans that conflict with it.

Does passing those checks establish romantic interest?

Let's work through what the evidence actually supports.

---

## Three different outputs

A recommendation is not a reply.

A reply is not a relationship.

“Reply observed” is data.

“They like Alex” is an inference.

HTTP 200: the request succeeded. That is not evidence of affection.

---

## Meet Alex

Fictional case · adult CS student.

Likes **board games and terrible puns**.

Free **Friday 5–7 pm** · **$20** outing budget · travels by **bus**.

Wants to meet someone, with the possibility of a relationship.

These are supplied facts. Add no biography to improve the score.

---

## Test two invitations

**Exercise assumptions:** each outing costs $12 in total. Bus times are unknown.

**A:** Friday, 6–6.30 pm.

**B:** Friday, 8–8.30 pm.

Alex is free 5–7 pm and has $20. Which option passes those checks?

What must be checked before calling it feasible?

---

## Apply the rule

Keep an option only if it is **inside 5–7 pm** and costs **at most $20**.

**A passes:** 6–6.30 pm is inside the window; $12 is within budget.

**B fails:** 8–8.30 pm is outside the window.

A is still provisional: bus travel must fit the available time too.

Passing a scheduling check does not predict a reply.

---

## Classify the evidence

**1.** Alex is free Friday 5–7 pm.

**2.** A reply is recorded in a synthetic message log.

**3.** “The recipient wants a relationship with Alex.”

Classify each as an **input**, an **observation**, or an **inference about hidden state**.

---

## Worked answer

**Input:** Alex's supplied availability.

**Observation:** a reply recorded in the synthetic log.

**Inference:** “They want a relationship with Alex.”

**Hidden state:** the recipient's actual motivation remains unknown.

The log does not distinguish a polite reply from an interested one.

---

## Romantic Turing Test

A: “Seeking synergistic engagement.”

B: “Board games, bad puns. Free Friday 5–7 pm to meet someone.”

Quote two phrases in B and match each to a supplied fact.

Which details would an actual invitation still need?

---

## Check the bio claims

**Board games / bad puns** → supplied interests.

**Friday 5–7 pm** → supplied availability.

**Meet someone** → supplied aim.

An invitation still needs a place, total cost and workable bus travel.

Natural wording earns no exemption from evidence.

---

## Strictly: “No Feelings”

Do not invent emotion labels in a dataset.

People still get to have feelings.

They can decline, leave or change their minds.

A refusal ends the interaction; it is not a temporary error to retry.

---

## Acquisition ethics

Use the supplied synthetic data.

Check provenance, permission, purpose and minimum fields.

No app scraping, private messages or real-user experiments.

Public visibility is not a harvesting licence.
