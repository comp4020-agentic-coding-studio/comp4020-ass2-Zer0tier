## Boundary debrief

**Supplied:** Alex's time and budget.

**Assumed:** the candidates, their scores and the one-profile display limit.

**Observed in a log:** which profile was actually shown.

**Hidden:** motivation and mutual interest. An eligible candidate is still a person who can decline.

## Why teach Elo here?

A small rating model makes **expectation, surprise and feedback** inspectable.

We can change one rule and reproduce every update.

Our binary outcomes are simulated comparison results.

They are not measurements of a person's attractiveness or an app's recovered algorithm.

---

## Five symbols, one update

**R:** current rating · **Q:** comparison rating.

**S:** simulated result, 0 or 1 · **K:** positive update size.

**E:** model expectation, between 0 and 1.

<div class="deck-formula">E = 1 / (1 + 10<sup>(Q − R) / 400</sup>)<br />R′ = R + K(S − E)</div>

---

---

## Work an equal-rating win

R = Q = **1200** · K = **32** · S = **1**.

E = 1 / (1 + 10⁰) = **0.5**.

Change = 32 × (1 − 0.5) = **+16**.

New rating = **1216**. Keep the expectation in the trace.

---

## Change one input at a time

Reset to R = Q = 1200 for each case.

**Loss, K = 32:** 1200 + 32(0 − 0.5) = **1184**.

**Win, K = 64:** 1200 + 64(1 − 0.5) = **1232**.

K changes responsiveness. A larger movement does not make the model more accurate.

---

## Surprise gets a larger update

R = **1200**, Q = **1600**, K = 32.

E = 1 / (1 + 10¹) = **1/11 ≈ 0.0909**.

Win: **1229.09**. Loss: **1197.09**.

Explain why the unexpected win moves the rating much more than the expected loss.

---

## Updates have state

Start at 1200. Win against 1200 → **1216**.

Then lose against 1200: recompute E using **1216**, not 1200.

E ≈ **0.5230** → final rating ≈ **1199.26**.

A win then a loss need not cancel. Round for display, not between steps.

## What counts as a result?

Here S is an explicit **simulated binary result**.

An unseen profile supplies no result.

An unanswered message is not automatically S = 0.

Before adapting a model, define its event, missing-data rule and observation window.

## Change the allocator · 6 min

Keep seeds **6 and 4**, a **1,000-exposure** budget and equal **10%** response probabilities.

Allocate **500 exposures each**, regardless of previous likes.

## Feedback debrief

Equal exposure → **50 expected new likes each**.

Cumulative likes become **56 and 54**: A's share ≈ **50.9%**.

The response probabilities stayed equal; the allocation rule changed.

Our proportional loop preserves a seed advantage. It does not prove that every feedback loop widens the share gap.

## A defensible rewrite

**1216** is the output of our equal-rating simulated win.

In the reversal fixture, A has **80 likes**, but B has the higher observed rate: **20% vs 8%**.

**13.3%** is reciprocal eligibility among 300 active synthetic profiles.

No platform score, bio effect or date probability was measured.
