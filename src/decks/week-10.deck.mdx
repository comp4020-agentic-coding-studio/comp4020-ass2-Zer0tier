## Absorbing is a counting convention

N and E freeze the **first endpoint reached** by a simulated path.

A real meeting can end after agreement. A person can later withdraw.

The observation record must retain those distinctions.

N is a narrow model endpoint; E is a legitimate ending. Neither grades a person.

## Source rows, destination columns

Use row vectors: **v(next) = vP**.

Component j = sum over i of **v[i] × P[i,j]**.

Read the old vector completely before replacing it.

[MIT's matrix lecture, slides 3–4](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-262-discrete-stochastic-processes-spring-2011/2fdbd4633466ba1429e7cc24bce37514_MIT6_262S11_lec07.pdf) supplies the method. The course supplies every probability.

## Answer · two different bugs

The proposed row sums to **1.10**. Returning N to 0.20 restores the baseline.

With the transposed update, initial C gives **(0.50, 0.30, 0, 0)**. Mass is lost.

This matrix's columns need not sum to one.

A consistent column-vector reformulation is possible; mixing conventions is a bug.

## A denominator can change the story

Unconditional N probability at step two: **0.32**.

Conditional on reaching N or E: **0.32 / 0.53 ≈ 0.6038**.

The second number excludes every transient path.

Neither is an observed dating rate. Label the horizon and denominator.

## Eventual absorption asks another question

From C/A, one-step transient retention is at most **0.70**.

After k steps, transient probability is at most **0.70ᵏ → 0**.

Let x and y be eventual N probabilities from C and A:

**x = 0.50x + 0.20y + 0.20**

**y = 0.30x + 0.30y + 0.10**

## Exercise · solve the endpoint probability

**6 minutes.** Rearrange the two first-step equations and solve for x and y.

## Answer · more horizons, more assumptions

Determinant **0.29**. x = **16/29 ≈ 0.551724**; y = **11/29 ≈ 0.379310**.

Extra N mass arrives after step two. This does not recommend waiting for agreement.

A closed C/A class makes this solve singular.

Extension: expected absorption from C is **90/29 steps**. Steps are not minutes.

## Answer · zero mass stays impossible

The C-row destinations are **C, A, N, E**.

The N-row destination must be **N**, even at u = 0.

Using ≤ can choose the first zero-probability cell and escape an absorbing state.

Reject u = 1. The draw contract is **[0,1)**.

---

## Specify the random stream

Teaching generator: unsigned 32-bit linear congruential generator.

Advance: **(1664525 × state + 1013904223) mod 2³²**.

Divide the new word by 2³². Seed **402010** gives first word **154701297**.

Use one draw per step, including after absorption. This generator is not cryptographic.

---

## Reset paths, keep the stream running

For each run: reset to **C**, take **two steps**, count the final state.

Do not reseed each path or carry the previous endpoint into the next path.

The ideal sampling model has independent paths. A pseudorandom stream approximates it.

Restarting the same seed for 1,000 and 10,000 runs creates nested samples.

## Exercise · reject a flaky test

Which cell disproves it in this fixture?

---

## Answer · repeatability is not convergence

C's error rises to **0.0008**. N's error is **0.18 percentage points**.

Reseeding every path repeats one outcome.

Under ideal independent indicators, standard deviation is **√(p(1−p)/n)**.

For N: about **0.01475 → 0.004665**. This scaling does not bound every seeded error.

## Find the missing 0.15

C → C → N changes from **0.10 to 0.05**: −0.05.

C → N → N changes from **0.20 to 0.10**: −0.10.

C → A → N remains **0.02**.

## Topics and eye contact are not consent flags

A topic transition can omit history, time remaining or a changed plan.

Adding states does not provide calibrated transition probabilities.

Do not infer attraction or discomfort from silence or eye-contact duration.

Disability and cultural difference are not anomaly scores. The A label is authored.

## Exercise · the bill has changed

**5 minutes.** The drink bill is **$14**, or its amount is unknown.

Can the existing $12 cost agreement allocate it?

Can the unresolved bill prevent either actor from leaving?

Is this stalemate a proven operating-system resource deadlock?

---

## Answer · review costs, retain the exit

Changed or unknown bill → **review**. Clarify terms and obtain a separate renewed agreement.

No automatic split, charge or obligation to stay follows.

[OSTEP §32.3](https://pages.cs.wisc.edu/~remzi/OSTEP/threads-bugs.pdf) requires four resource-deadlock conditions. Our waiting exercise is a limited analogy.

A person is not a lock to acquire.

## A later withdrawal remains possible

Two explicit agreements → **agreed**.

A later explicit withdrawal → **declined**, retaining the event history.

Do not retry a declined invitation. Leaving ends this meeting record even with an unresolved bill.

Simulated N remains absorbing because it freezes a first endpoint, not a person's future wishes.
